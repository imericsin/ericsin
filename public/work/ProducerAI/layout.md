## The Challenge

Producer.AI is a music production app built around LLMs at its core, aiming to challenge the accessibility of music creation and the antiquated processes tied to it.

I was brought in to help lay out foundational design systems while designing better UX for key jobs to be done. A full brand refresh ran in parallel, ensuring a consistent throughline across the brand's experience—both in marketing and in-app.

This product was acquired by Google in 2026, repositioned as "Google Flow Music".

::03_2COL
From Riffusion to Producer

Initially named "Riffusion," the team and I rebuilt the identity around "Producer"—a name that better centered the core problem the app was built to solve.

::04_TEXT

## Brand Inspiration

We leaned heavily into the origin of music—drawing on artifacts and visual languages that called back to a nostalgic view of musical history. By blending modern experience design with notes of playfulness, we created a simple, scalable brand language that could find its moments.

## Product-First Branding

Beyond crafting a visual language the team could use across marketing collateral, the brand needed to be simple enough to apply across an existing infrastructure.

Overhauling the visual language with a new, opinionated design system brought consistency to a UX that had been missing it—and gave the rest of the team a faster path to ideate, refine, and build.

## Visioncrafting, Holistically

Designing a brand in parallel with product work isn't just about systems thinking—it's also about vision. I took advantage of the team's willingness to explore, shaping both product and brand direction through design prototyping and roadmaps.

The result was a brand that felt modern and premium, yet still approached music creation as something fun and collaborative—one that uplifted the process of inspiration over pure output.

## Success Found in Acquisition

Ultimately, the brand was successful in getting key stakeholders genuinely proud of what they were putting out. It rallied the existing team—engineers, builders, and everyone in between—and resonated with the product's user base as well.

The work paid off in the end, culminating in an acquisition by Google—the product has since been rebranded as Google Flow Music.
