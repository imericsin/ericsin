## The Challenge

Mosaic Finance is a B2B SaaS platform for FP&A professionals, replacing Excel and disconnected spreadsheets as the source of truth for budgeting, headcount, and top-line planning. The product was live with solid revenue, but users treated it as a dumping ground rather than a place to collaborate.

I joined the team as a Sr. Product Designer in 2023 and segmented my time into two chapters—one initially from a place of tactical impact, and later from the perspective of strategic impact.

::03_FULL

::04_TEXT
## Tactical Impact
Design had been a recurring blocker for engineering; within six months, that stopped. Production-to-Figma parity freed the team to think through problems instead of scrambling.

I also brought prototyping skills the team lacked, running usability tests—like a hotkey study benchmarking FP&A workflows against Excel—to validate ideas ahead of deep domain expertise. The C-suite noticed the team moving faster.

::05_3COL

## Visioncrafting with Design Systems

After unblocking engineering with a production-accurate design system, we focused our sights on improving the experience through a second, vision-oriented DS that we continuously challenged through UXR sessions.

::06_TEXT
## Strategic Impact
When our beloved Head of Design left mid-year, I recognized a trust gap forming between leadership and design. Without a clear strategic design leader at the helm, I took initiative by validating stalled initiatives instead of defaulting to order-taking. 

One of those was a two-way Google Sheets sync — risky and resource-intensive, greenlit to close a collaboration gap. Vision prototyping and UXR validation uncovered the real job to be done, leading to a faster, lower-risk concept: Shared Workspaces, built on existing components.

## Results & Learnings
When looking for ways to be impactful at a new organization, you can relieve a lot of pressure off of your team if you recognize the non-context heavy options and do the "dirty work" of building, assisting, and being overly communicative.

On the strategic side—our executives who'd worried design couldn't operate without its former leader came away convinced otherwise. The org abandoned the sync work for Shared Workspaces, and Metric Builder (another vision project) gave the roadmap a clearer, less risky direction it hadn't had considered.

The lesson: in a leadership vacuum, identify the highest source of risk, form a hypothesis for the fix, and let customer conversations drive the solutions.
