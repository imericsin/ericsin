## Overview

You.com started as a B2C search engine, complete with a dated, rainbow-hued logo that no longer fit the space it was entering. With ChatGPT reshaping user expectations around chat-based interfaces, we needed to modernize the product and rebrand around a new B2B consulting model—without losing the loyal user base and ARR the original product had already earned.

I worked alongside product, marketing, and engineering to ensure there was a clear throughline across orgs through design, ultimately aligning the business from a visual design and user experience perspective.

## Reimagining the Product & Brand

We rebuilt the product experience from the ground up, moving from a legacy search interface to a modern chat console in line with what OpenAI, Slack, and ChatGPT had normalized. 

The initiative was daunting in scope—design had to overhaul nearly every touchpoint of the business across brand, marketing, and product, from system modals, chat interfaces, and IA components to sign-in screens, marketing webpages, and social assets.

## Designing for Two: Consumers and Businesses

A challenge we faced was prioritizing work between our bet towards a B2B model, while pushing the experience for our existing userbase. Through customer conversations and direct user feedback, we identified overlapping use-cases that we could build workflows around.

One of these initiatives were "Projects"—a space for users to do work with contained knowledge bases, focused Agents, and collaboration.

## Design as a Sales Weapon

Alongside the consumer-facing work, conversations with prospective B2B customers gave me valuable insight into how businesses at scale were forming appetites for LLM integrations. 

I worked directly with leadership to rapidly prototype, pitch, and sell tailored AI solutions—resulting in over $1.6M in ARR ($100K Avg. Contract Value) within the first quarter of launching our B2B model.

## Results and Learnings

Amongst our existing users, the work was well received enough. To our B2B customers, the work was immediately felt and impactful to our bottom line.

By embedding myself closely with key stakeholders in the organization, I was always able to find positions where I could place myself to be impactful and find a direct route to fiscal value generation. 

Although I look back and see so many things I could improve, one of the things I'm most proud of is being able to look at that +$1.6M number and know that I was a direct contributor to it. I don't know many people who can say those things confidently—espcially amongst designers.
